## 2026-08-10 — Establish propagation, attribution, implementation, and scarcity doctrine

- **Decision ID:** SAS-DEC-2026-08-10-01
- **Change type:** doctrine change
- **Affected document:** `docs/FOUNDATION_DOCTRINE.md`
- **Governance effect:** strengthens governance
- **Status:** proposed for merge

### Decision

Add a methodology-level doctrine distinguishing four mechanisms by which a sovereign conceptual asset accumulates value:

1. propagation as distribution;
2. attribution as authority;
3. implementation as leverage; and
4. scarce identity — especially the exact domain — as a non-replicable coordination point.

Adopt the governing sentence:

> **Propagation is distribution. Attribution is authority. Implementation is leverage. The domain is scarcity.**

Also institute an explicit tracked-version reference in the Foundation Doctrine without asserting an undocumented historical version sequence.

### Rationale

A portfolio that deliberately builds conceptual and category assets must distinguish public conceptual propagation from loss of strategic control. Treating every copied idea as value leakage would encourage unnecessary enclosure and weaken distribution. Treating every public disclosure as harmless would create the opposite failure: uncontrolled release of implementation advantage or protectable know-how.

The doctrine therefore separates what can gain value through public propagation from what gains value through canonical attribution, superior implementation, accumulated evidence, brand stewardship, and scarce ownership surfaces.

The amendment does not assert that domain ownership creates trademark rights, that timestamped publication automatically creates legal exclusivity, or that every public technical contribution automatically qualifies as prior art in every jurisdiction.

### Expected cross-asset effect

The principle applies portfolio-wide, particularly to category-defining and doctrine-driven assets whose authority benefits from citation, reuse, and external implementation.
