# Isotely — Historical Meaning and Modern Category Reference

**Version:** 0.3 proposal · 2026-10-25  
**Review status:** Internal research, not a legal standard or a cleared trademark.

## Original word, bounded historical statement

The English word **Isotely** has a recorded historical lexicographic life associated with Greek *isoteleia*. Historical accounts associate the latter with privileged fiscal equality for certain non-citizens and not with automatic full citizenship. The contemporary phrase **“Equal Treatment. Without Citizenship.”** is a modern editorial interpretation of this distinction. Do not claim it is a precise Ancient Greek definition or that all civic rights were equalized.

Historical entry points:
- [University of Milan — Lexicon of Citizenship](https://politeia.unimi.it/en/databases/lexicon-of-citizenship/)
- [Oxford English Dictionary — isotely, n.](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/isotely_n) (access may be restricted)

## The modern method (proposed)

**Legal-treatment parity** is a narrowly scoped relationship, evaluated with evidence, between legal-status profiles with respect to **one defined legal treatment**. A treatment can be an entitlement, obligation, restriction, exemption, or legal protection.

Minimum relation key:

```
jurisdiction + governing level
status_subject + comparator_status
right_or_obligation + specific legal scope
conditions + exclusions + operative legal sources
effective_on + source_checked_at
relationship_classification + uncertainty + review_state
```

No one country scores another; no single treatment stands for total civic equality; no status label is assumed comparable across all national legal systems.

### Allowed analytical outcomes

| Code | Meaning | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| `equal_on_defined_rule` | Equal on this particular legal rule | Not equality in all related matters |
| `conditional` | Equality or difference depends on explicit conditions | Conditions must be sourced |
| `different_legal_basis` | Related protection flows from different legal sources | Not proof that a right is missing |
| `different_entitlement` | The specific right differs under the rule examined | Don't infer an overall status ranking |
| `inapplicable` | Instrument or relation does not apply | Not equivalent to prohibited |
| `unresolved` | Evidence lacks sufficiency or interpretative authority | Cannot coerce to a binary answer |

Record **which** legal instrument governs, **when** it applies, and **who** reviewed its interpretation.

### Point-in-time versus change event

A research record about present rules is **not** a verified historical change. A `ParityChange` requires confirmed pre-change and post-change sources, effective dates, relation key, relevant exceptions and legal-review sign-off. The three Canadian examples in `index.html` are only point-in-time research and are not change events.

## Brand separation

`Isotely` identifies a proposed source, steward, reference and commercial identity. A broader conceptual discussion of legal-treatment parity should not be portrayed as exclusively owned because it relates to this brand. Assess trademark registration and likely confusion in the actual target markets and services; do not assume a dictionary word is registrable or unregistrable without analysis.

## Research examples in current preview

1. **Canada — livelihood mobility**: Charter s.6(2)(b), read subject to s.6(3)–(4), extends the specified protection to citizens and permanent residents. [Official text](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/page-12.html).
2. **Canada — entry/remain**: Citizen protection under Charter s.6(1), resident right under IRPA s.27(1), and conditions including IRPA s.28. Do not code a permanent resident's right as absent. [Charter](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/page-12.html) · [IRPA 27](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/i-2.5/section-27.html) · [IRPA 28](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-2.5/section-28.html).
3. **Canada — federal voting**: Federal electoral voting requires Canadian citizenship. [Elections Canada](https://www.elections.ca/content.aspx?dir=bkg&document=ec90518&lang=e&section=vot).

**All three:** research-only, no independent qualified legal review, no completeness claim, no asserted verified law-change event. A link to a government source does not substitute for professional legal interpretation.

## Novelty restraint

Comparative legal data, migrant-rights datasets, and legislative monitoring are established fields. Isotely must earn category credibility through analytical precision, source traceability, maintained records, independently observable use, and measured buyer demand; no declaration of being the “first” or “world standard.”
