## Editorial banner
**Equal Treatment. Without Citizenship.**

This phrase expresses the intellectual tension at the heart of Isotely: a non-citizen may obtain comparable legal treatment for a **specified** entitlement or obligation while remaining legally a non-citizen. It is not universal equality, automatic eligibility, or the full transfer of citizenship rights.

## Lexical versus modern meaning
Ancient Greek **isoteleia** described fiscal equality privileges for certain non-citizens; it did not itself name a universal modern legal comparison framework. This new framework is an **explicit modern extension**, not a recovered ancient science or an unqualified historical translation.

Primary academic references: [University of Milan — isoteles/isoteleia](https://politeia.unimi.it/en/databases/lexicon-of-citizenship/) and [Academy of Athens — Isoteleia](https://humanitiesthesaurus.academyofathens.gr/HUMANITIES-THESAURUS/en/page/?uri=https%3A%2F%2Fhumanitiesthesaurus.academyofathens.gr%2Fdyas-resource%2FConcept%2F2085).

## Controlled contemporary definition
**An Isotely treatment relationship** is a bounded comparison of the legal treatment applicable to a specified non-citizen status against an explicitly defined comparator status regarding **one** right, obligation, privilege, eligibility rule or legal effect, in an identified jurisdiction, at an identified effective time and subject to identified conditions and exceptions.

The object of measurement is **the relationship**, not a country's aggregate moral or policy score.

### Required record dimensions
- Jurisdiction and competent legal authority.
- Non-citizen legal profile and specific comparator.
- The single entitlement, obligation or legal effect at issue.
- Source instruments, exact provisions, scope, exceptions and competing authorities.
- Effective date and time of source verification, distinguished.
- Relationship category and textual rationale.
- Unresolved facts, review grade, qualified sign-off and change provenance.

### Relationship vocabulary
- **Equal under defined rule:** the cited norm expressly covers both profiles in the limited subject matter.
- **Conditionally equal:** equality turns on explicit conditions.
- **Different legal foundations:** materially related protections exist but attach under different norms.
- **Different entitlement:** an explicitly grounded difference in the specified entitlement.
- **Inapplicable:** the instrument does not govern the relationship.
- **Unresolved:** conflicting or insufficient evidence; no binary inference.

**Constraint:** no conclusion may be inferred solely from a missing citation, a country label, or a historical analogy. A comparator that changes scope also changes the question.

## Research laboratory: Canada (limited)
The site preview illustrates three distinct, **uncertified** points in time: (1) section 6(2)(b) of the Canadian Charter as to livelihood mobility for citizens and permanent residents, with section 6(3)–(4) conditions; (2) differing constitutional and statutory bases of entry/residence in Charter s.6(1) and IRPA s.27, not a denial of permanent-resident protection; and (3) citizenship requirements for federal voting per Elections Canada. ## Scope governance
The working framework is global in scope but jurisdiction-bound in evidence. It does not imply worldwide coverage. Isotely is the source identifier; the general relationship should not be marketed as legally exclusive ownership of an ordinary abstract concept. Historical claims and modern proposals must always be separately labelled.
