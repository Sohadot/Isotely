Equal Treatment.
Without Citizenship.

Isotely examines how a non-citizen can receive treatment comparable to that of a citizen for a defined right or obligation—without acquiring citizenship itself.

Our proposed framework makes this relationship explicit: who is compared, what treatment is measured, under which law, subject to which conditions, and as of what date.

01 / The meaning already existed

A word with history.
A question for the present.

A rare term is more valuable when its intellectual origin is handled precisely.

Equal treatment in a particular matter need not mean equal civic membership.

The historical Greek isoteleia referred to privileges of fiscal equality granted to certain non-citizens. Scholarly lexicographical work describes comparable financial obligations to citizens; it does not establish that the beneficiary acquired full citizenship or that every legal right became equal.

Our contemporary application—comparing treatment across modern legal statuses—is a proposed conceptual extension, not a claim that ancient Greeks developed a modern global legal database.

Historical references: University of Milan, Lexicon of Citizenship · Academy of Athens, Humanities Thesaurus.

02 / The modern reference

Where does treatment converge—and where does it diverge?

The subject of an Isotely comparison is a specific legal-treatment relationship, not a blanket judgment about a person, nationality, country, or immigration regime.

01 / DEFINE

Whose status?

Identify the non-citizen profile and the precise comparator: citizen, resident or another legally defined class as appropriate to the research question.

02 / BOUND

Which treatment?

Specify one right, financial obligation, privilege or limitation. Record eligibility, legal foundations, exceptions and applicable conditions.

03 / EVIDENCE

Under what rule?

Record authoritative sources, jurisdiction, effective date, verification date and review state. Do not infer equality or exclusion from missing evidence.

Assessment vocabulary: equal under the specified rule · conditionally equal · different legal foundation · different entitlement · inapplicable · unresolved. An unknown relationship stays unresolved.
03 / Bounded research examples

Evidence, not a country ranking.

The first research exercise compares Canadian permanent residents with Canadian citizens under three separate rules. The illustrations below are limited readings of identified public legal sources. They have not received external qualified legal review.

CA / 001

Livelihood mobility

Shared rule, subject to conditions

Charter section 6(2)(b) describes a mobility-related livelihood protection for both citizens and permanent residents. This does not establish parity in every job, license or benefit.

05 / Intellectual integrity

The name is an origin, not a legal conclusion.

The historical scope of isoteleia and the proposed Isotely method are intentionally distinguished. “Equal Treatment. Without Citizenship.” is a modern editorial proposition, not a guarantee of equal treatment or a historical claim of universal rights.
