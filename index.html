Equal Treatment.
Without Citizenship.

Isotely begins with an attested historical distinction: a noncitizen could receive defined equal treatment without becoming a citizen. Today, that distinction invites a precise global question: equal in which right or obligation, compared with whom, where, and under what conditions?

01 / Historical provenance

The word preceded the framework.

Isotely is not a new name invented for a market trend. Its historical lineage is the Greek isoteleia, associated with particular equality in fiscal obligations for some noncitizens.

Equality in a defined treatment need not confer the whole status of citizenship.

In Greek civic practice, isoteleia could give privileged noncitizens fiscal treatment equivalent to citizens. At Athens, privileged resident foreigners could be exempt from the metoikion tax. The full extent of other privileges is historically debated; the modern model must not portray this as universal ancient equality.

Historical source: University of Milan · Lexicon of Citizenship · Isoteles / Isoteleia. Dictionary record: Oxford English Dictionary · Isotely (availability may depend on access).
02 / The modern relation

A relation, not a country score.

A proposed legal-treatment comparison must name the exact noncitizen status, its comparator, one particular right or obligation, the jurisdiction, applicable legal conditions, authoritative sources, and effective time. The result must express uncertainty rather than hide it.

01 — PERSON
Defined statusA specific legal category, not “migrants” as a whole; plus an explicit comparator such as a citizen under matched conditions.
02 — TREATMENT
One entitlementA particular right, burden, benefit, or exception. Equality in one field cannot prove equality in another.
03 — EVIDENCE
Jurisdiction and timePinpointed legal sources, conditions, effective dates, review status, and unresolved interpretations.
Jurisdiction × defined legal status × comparator × specific treatment × conditions × effective date → bounded assessment

Possible findings include: equivalent under a specified rule; conditional; different entitlement; different legal foundation; outside the instrument's scope; disputed or unresolved. None implies a global ranking of states or of human worth.
