01 / Historical meaning

The word existed
before the project.

Equality in a particular treatment need not confer equality of civic status.

Historically, Greek isoteleia described a privileged fiscal position associated with certain non-citizens. It did not by itself confer the complete rights of citizenship. Our modern research starts with this bounded distinction.

The headline “Equal Treatment. Without Citizenship.” is a modern interpretive proposition, not a verbatim translation or a claim that every right was equal in antiquity.

02 / Modern framework

A precisely bounded comparison.

We extend the historical question into contemporary legal analysis without presenting our method as an ancient doctrine or an official global standard.

A legal-treatment parity relation compares the treatment of a defined non-citizen status against an expressly identified comparator, regarding a specific right or obligation, in a particular jurisdiction and at a given effective time.
I · STATUS

Who is compared?

Identify both legal-status profiles. Do not collapse “resident,” “citizen,” or “migrant” into universal legal classes.

II · TREATMENT

What is compared?

Specify one right, obligation, exemption, or protection, together with exceptions, prerequisites, and legal foundations.

III · EVIDENCE

What can be concluded?

State governing source, applicable date, verification status, and explicit uncertainty. An unknown is not an inequality.
