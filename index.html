01 / Lexical provenance

The word is historic.
The inquiry is global.

In ancient Greek civic life, isoteleia referred to limited privileges for certain noncitizens, particularly in fiscal treatment, without conferring citizenship or universal equality.

Parity in treatment need not mean identity of status.

History is the anchor.
Evidence is the authority.

The University of Milan’s Lexicon of Citizenship describes isoteles / isoteleia as a privilege by which Greek states could place noncitizens on the same footing as citizens regarding financial obligations. In Athens, this included fiscal privileges for certain resident foreigners.

The modern category proposed here is an explicit extension: we examine documented treatment relationships across contemporary legal statuses and specific rights or duties. The ancient term is not represented as having meant this entire modern framework.

02 / Category method

A measurable unit of parity.

A parity assessment examines a defined noncitizen status against an explicit comparator—often a citizen—for one particular right or obligation, under controlling laws and conditions. The record, not an entire country, receives a bounded assessment.

AXIS 01

Status & comparator

Which exact noncitizen status is being assessed, and against which reference status? Avoid “foreigners” as an undifferentiated category.

AXIS 02

Right & conditions

What precisely is being compared? Distinguish the legal foundation of a right from the practical conditions of exercising it.

AXIS 03

Source & time

What operative legislation, judgments and exceptions support the finding, and when did each rule apply or change?
