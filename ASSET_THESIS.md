## Category identity
**Isotely — Equal Treatment. Without Citizenship.**

Isotely is an attested historical English word associated with Greek *isoteleia*, which conveyed a bounded kind of equal treatment for some noncitizens, notably regarding fiscal obligations, without conferring citizenship. The modern global comparison framework is our proposed **extension** of that history, not an assertion that the ancient word described contemporary immigration law.

**Modern category (descriptive):** legal-status treatment parity — a sourced, jurisdiction-specific relation between an expressly defined noncitizen status and an expressly identified comparator (normally citizen) **for one right or obligation**, subject to conditions, exceptions and dates.

**Not claimed:** universal equality, relative national virtue, citizenship equivalence, a new legal right, sole ownership of the historical word, or official standardization.

## Global reach, narrow evidence
Worldwide category scope is a design choice, *not* a claim of completed international legal coverage. Every assessment requires: jurisdiction, legal status, comparator, specific entitlement/duty, operative conditions, authoritative legal sources with pinpoint citations, effective date, verification date, confidence/review state, and a verdict that can be **unresolved**.

Allowed scoped outcomes include equality under the specified rule, conditional treatment, different legal foundations, different entitlement, inapplicability, legal dispute and insufficient evidence. An absent source is never proof of absence of a right.
